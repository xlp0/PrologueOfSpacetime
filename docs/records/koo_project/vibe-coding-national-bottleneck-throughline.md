# Vibe Coding the National Bottleneck

## Insight
Prof. Putu's method, distilled: take a **national bottleneck** → use AI / vibe coding to build a **thin working layer fast** → turn the system into something **measurable** → build locally with **domain experts**, not career engineers. This is the throughline that connects GeoWatch, AI Voice Chat, Math Game, and ClassOS into one coherent state-building agenda rather than four random demos.

The method matters because it answers "why are these kids playing with Lego?" The Lego rocket is not the point. The point is: with AI + the right process + the right environment, Indonesia can train people to **build** now — without waiting for the formal school system to produce builders. The weekly changing projects exist because Ben wants them to extract the **meta-skill**: take an unfamiliar problem → break it down → use tools/AI → produce an output → reflect on what worked → carry that process into the next problem. PKC is where that process gets captured so the next team starts from a higher base.

The political pitch to government: don't assume only foreign vendors, old professors, or giant firms can build systems. Young local Indonesians, if structured properly, can build useful things too. State-capacity question + sovereignty question: who builds the systems? Who understands them? Who can maintain them? Who owns the knowledge? The kids are not demonstrating "govtech" itself; they are demonstrating a **production model for talent**. "If we can take young Indonesians and turn them into people who can build working things with AI and open tools, then Indonesia can create the human engine needed for govtech, edtech, and other national systems."

Capability-building theater and flagship national AI demo are two layers happening at once. Both are necessary; neither is sufficient alone.
