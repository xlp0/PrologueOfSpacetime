# GK Media Group / Lens — IQS & Information Control Researcher

## Insight
**Lens** (gkmediagroup.com / lens.cx) is building the "Infrastructure for Information" — tools that surface the hidden influences behind the content people consume. Flagship product: the **Information Quality Standard (IQS)**, which analyzes news articles, research, and other media at a **sentence level**, identifying logical fallacies, emotionally manipulative language, financial conflicts of interest, and other factors that shape how information is framed and received. Self-described as "the Bloomberg Terminal for information" / "the nutrition label for information." Backed by $80K pre-seed, seeking $3M seed.

The **Information Control Researcher (ICR)** role: hands-on, stress-testing the IQS against real-world content. Run the tool against news/opinion/academic/financial content; evaluate accuracy, relevance, fairness of scores and sentence-level breakdowns; identify edge cases (misclassification, over-flagging, missing influences); document bugs, inconsistencies, UX friction clearly; provide qualitative feedback on whether outputs feel intuitive and trustworthy; collaborate with team to iterate on rubrics and scoring criteria. Essentially **AI evaluator + media-bias researcher + QA tester** wearing a trench coat.

Founder: **James G. E. Knurbein** (CEO, NYC). CTO: **Michael Haynes** (Buffalo NY; ex-Ingram Micro System Architect; founder of Haynes Technology Group LLC; lead dev of Neutron AI for iOS/Android/Windows/Xbox). 

**Yellow flags:** very small public footprint, no named customers / case studies / traction metrics, site copy heavy on grand language ("infrastructure for information," "Bloomberg/Moody's for media," "credibility standard") but light on hard evidence. Michael Haynes not cleanly verifiable (common name). Recent CTO start date (Apr 2026, 3 months). 

**Green flags:** consistent story across Wellfound + official site; role itself coherent and fits an early-stage media/AI/research workflow; "Information Control Researcher / IQS evaluator" is weird but plausible startup-weird, not nonsense-weird.

**Verdict:** legit enough to continue, verify before emotional investment. Reply briefly (2-line acknowledgment, don't ask 12 due-diligence questions before they even schedule). For the interview: 90-second story (AI evaluator/QA/analytical operator), 3 examples (hard edge case, calibration fix, "obvious" answer was wrong), principled critique of IQS (sentence-level misses context; "emotional language" isn't always manipulation; rubric designer bias; financial conflicts relevant but not automatically disqualifying; strong claims need source/context weighting, not just rhetoric detection). Don't say "I will save democracy with sentence scoring" — sounds like a TED Talk hostage.
