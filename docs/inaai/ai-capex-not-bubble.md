# AI Capex Is Not a Bubble

## Insight
The finance/research analyst's argument: the AI infrastructure buildout is **not a bubble** — it's the necessary capex for a new tech revolution — though individual stock valuations may be. The distinction matters: buildout-as-input vs. stock-as-bet-on-future-cash-flow.

**Why now — the demand side:**

- ChatGPT kicked off the LLM era → specialist → generalist models → now physical AI.
- Compute + AI maturing together unlocks robotics/physical AI.
- AGI race is a **prisoner's dilemma**: in AGI, 2nd place = last place. Giants must spend regardless of cash-flow pressure.
- Training demand dominates today (rapid model iteration, GPT-5-class models). **Inference** is the real long-term demand black hole: train once on 100k GPUs for 3 months, then serve billions of users forever.
- Each model generation needs 10–100× the compute of the previous.

**The capex numbers:**

- **Stargate** (OpenAI + SoftBank + Oracle): ~$500B planned (≈ Thailand's annual GDP). Phase 1 = 400k GPUs, ~$100B.
- Amazon capex: $131B (2025) → $200B (2026).
- **CoreWeave** (US AI compute-rental newcomer): $100M (2022) → $35.3B (2025).
- **Nebius**: signed a $27B deal with Meta recently.
- Cloud capacity = "land reserves" of this era. Strategy risk of under-investing > financial risk of over-investing.

The shovel-seller framework: target the essential physical links (compute, network, cooling, PCB, power) that the industry is *forced* to keep upgrading no matter who wins the model war. Goldman Sachs estimates **75–80% of the $1T+ AI capex goes to compute hardware + network gear** — see [[ai-server-anatomy-h100]] for the breakdown.

The "Avengers" counter-alliance (UALink) is the structural threat to Nvidia's lock-in — see [[nvidia-tax-vs-ualink-alliance]].
