# Ben Koo Capability-Building Method

## Insight
Prof. Ben Koo's actual obsession, distilled from the Govtech AI convo: **teach the process, make kids build across different projects, capture the process so it compounds, create a machine for producing builders.** Not "kid made rocket" — "kid became someone who can build." The weekly changing projects exist specifically to force extraction of the meta-skill: take an unfamiliar problem → break it into steps → use AI/tools → produce an output → reflect on what worked → carry that process into the next problem.

Ben's framing language: "you are writing your own constitution," "you program your own universe," "everything must fit into one continuous story," "there's no map." Strip the drama off and what he means is: **don't just do projects randomly — create a system where every project leaves behind a map so the next person can continue.** That map / constitution / memory / structure is what PKC is trying to be.

The method, as a repeatable loop: (1) take a problem; (2) break it into modules; (3) use AI/tools to fill skill gaps; (4) produce a working result; (5) document the process so others can repeat it. This is useful for govtech because public-sector problems are also just messy projects that need decomposition, execution, iteration, documentation. The Lego rocket and the math game are not the end product — they are proof that the method works. The argument to government or investors is not "look, children built a Lego rocket, invest now" (that would be clownish). It is: **"Indonesia can build domestic execution capability"** — not just theory, not just policy talk, not just imported consultants, but actual local people who can build systems quickly using AI + open tools + a repeatable process.

The method explains why Ben Koo was unimpressed by Ilham in 2021 when Ilham was focused on Python and data science — people from MIT Engineering Systems / Knowledge Management / Innovation Systems / Organizational Design often view coding as a tool rather than the main event. They care about incentives, institutions, information flow, organizational architecture more than whether someone can build another classifier. Five years later, Ilham's essays (information organization, labor market matching, institutions, knowledge systems, decision systems) sound much closer to Ben Koo's world than to the average data scientist's world.

## Context
Came up 2026-06-24 (Govtech AI in Indonesia). Ilham was unpacking what his father's friend had been building and why the kids/Lego/math-game projects mattered for govtech.
