# RLHF Calibration 70% → 90% Across 26 QAs

## Insight
The single most-referenced positioning fact across all of Ilham's 2026 job applications: **"Improved calibration alignment from 70% to 90% across 26 Quality Analysts in one month."** This is the anchor metric — it appears in cover letters, Wellfound "stand out" boxes, resume bullets, interview prep, and LinkedIn drafts. Treat as the canonical evidence piece.

Why it works: it's specific, quantified, defensible, and unusual. Most applicants have vague "improved processes" or "managed teams." Ilham has a measurable jump in evaluator consistency over a defined population in a defined timeframe. For AI-evaluation companies (Braintrust, LangWatch, Lens, AI Deploy Labs), this metric is immediately legible — they live this pain daily. The litmus test for whether a contact at an AI-eval company is worth messaging: when they read this sentence, do they immediately understand why it's impressive? If yes, good target. If not, wrong target.

**Resume bullet variants by target role:**
- *AI-eval / RLHF roles:* "Improved calibration alignment from 70% to 90% across 26 Quality Analysts within one month, strengthening consistency in multilingual LLM evaluation and post-training review workflows."
- *QA roles:* "Improved calibration agreement from 70% to 90% across 26 Quality Analysts within one month by aligning evaluation criteria, reducing review variance, and strengthening quality control standards."
- *Analytics / operations:* "Improved calibration alignment from 70% to 90% across 26 Quality Analysts, supported training programs with 50+ contributors, and produced quality analytics to identify bottlenecks and improve performance."

The parenthetical context matters: across 26 QAs (population), in one month (timeframe), at Invisible Technologies (employer). Without those qualifiers, the number floats. With them, it's anchored. The framing principle: don't inflate the metric across roles — translate the *same* underlying achievement into the target role's language. QA recruiters want "evaluation criteria, reducing review variance, quality control standards." AI-eval recruiters want "multilingual LLM evaluation, post-training review workflows." Same fact, different vocabulary.
