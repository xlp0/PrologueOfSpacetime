# Presentation Strategy: Pitching to He Mengxin (UGREEN)

> *"This is not a pitch for a board game. It is a showcase of a pedagogical engine and computational simulation that brings his own intellectual frameworks to life, while proving the absolute necessity of the hardware ecosystem his company builds."*

## 1. The Core Objective & Tone
When presenting to He Mengxin—a mechanical engineer turned executive who led UGREEN to an IPO, and the intellectual mentor who inspired the "Cubical Logic Model" (CLM)—the presentation must be **punchy, executive-level, and philosophically grounded**. 

Do not make it "pale" or overly academic. Focus intensely on **what we have built** (the engine) and **how we teach** (the curriculum), tying everything to hardware resilience and economic optimization.

---

## 2. The Hook: Bringing "The Trinity" Full Circle
Begin by acknowledging his intellectual legacy. 
* **The Script:** "Years ago at Tsinghua, you introduced the 'Trinity' historical framework—History of People, Technology, and Institutions. That philosophy birthed the Cubical Logic Model (CLM). Today, I want to show you how we took those abstract concepts, built them into a computational engine, and created a playable simulation."

---

## 3. How to Present "What We Have Done" (The Engine)
He needs to see the concrete systems we've engineered, not just the lore. Present our accomplishments in terms of **systems design and infrastructure**:

1. **The Game as a Computational Engine:** We built an RPG where an LLM acts as the Dungeon Master (The Computational SSoT). It doesn't just tell a story; it processes player decisions as mathematical functions and outputs **MVP Cards** representing the new state of reality.
2. **Replacing Biology with Infrastructure (The UGREEN Synergy):** We completely removed traditional D&D stats (Strength, Dexterity). Instead, a player's power is defined by their **Infrastructure Stats**: Energy (ENE), Hardware (HDW), Communication (COM), and Software (SFW). 
    * *The Pitch here is critical:* "Mengxin, in this simulation, human stamina doesn't save you. Having a reliable 12V battery, an offline server, and clean data transfer saves you. The game proves that in the modern epoch, survival relies on the exact ecosystem of hardware UGREEN builds."
3. **The Economic Optimization Sandbox:** We created a 30-day deluge scenario in real-world Bali that forces players into make-or-buy analysis, systemic cost reduction, and resource decentralization using physical Rupiah (IDR). 

---

## 4. How to Present "The Curriculum" (The Pedagogy)
Do not present the game's rules; present the **7-Layer Pedagogical Architecture** hidden within the gameplay. Show him how the game acts as a Trojan Horse for sovereign thinking:

* **Layer 1: Survival (Resource Management):** Players must budget time and finite resources under extreme pressure.
* **Layer 2: Economic (Systemic Cost Reduction):** Players learn opportunity cost and economies of scale (e.g., crafting a server from components vs. buying one retail).
* **Layer 3: Computational (Functions & Types):** Players learn that every action is a "typed function." Vague inputs yield errors; precise inputs execute successfully.
* **Layer 4: Linguistic (Speech Acts):** They learn to use language as precise computational instructions (Prompt Engineering). 
* **Layer 5: Cultural (Language as Leverage):** We built Balinese language proficiency into the economy, teaching that cultural respect generates trust and tangible economic discounts.
* **Layer 6: Systemic (Emergence):** Players learn decentralization. Centralizing all servers in Denpasar means one earthquake wipes out their Software (SFW) stat. 
* **Layer 7: Philosophical (Protocol Truth):** They learn that truth isn't dictated by authority, but emerges from following a shared verification protocol.
