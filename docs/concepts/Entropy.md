# Entropy

> A measure of the level of freedom, alternative configurations, or uncompressed noise in a physical, informational, or architectural system.

## 1. Definition

In statistical mechanics and information theory, **Entropy** quantifies the uncertainty or dispersion of a system's states. In a resource-bounded setting, **Time-Bounded Entropy** ($H_T$) represents the implicit noise, side-effects, and operational friction that resists compression.

### 1.1 The Energy-SNR-Entropy Framework
Entropy is integrated into the unified framework for information density:

$$I_{\text{density}} = \rho_E \times \text{SNR} \times e^{-\alpha H}$$

where:
* **Energy Density ($\rho_E$)**: The density of computational update activities.
* **Signal-to-Noise Ratio (SNR)**: The proportion of policy-relevant, useful signal.
* **Entropy ($H$)**: The dispersion of states relative to a chosen partition of the state space.

### 1.2 Entropy as Opportunity Generator
While classical thermodynamics associates entropy increase with decay, information physics reframes it as the **expansion of the opportunity space**. Each new configuration generated by entropic expansion represents a latent pattern (opportunity) that an adaptive system can exploit.

## 2. In the Prologue and the 3E Framework

Entropy serves as the primary metric for **Efficiency** (the second dimension of the **[[3E Framework]]**):

* **Efficiency Metric**: Efficiency answers "Can we do it within budget?" by measuring the information loss or entropic dissipation during a transformation. A system is efficient when it minimizes entropy generation ($\Delta H_T \to \min$).

* **Leinster's Entropy-Magnitude Link**: Under Tom Leinster's category-theoretic framework, entropy is formulated as log-diversity, directly linking Shannon entropy to the **[[Magnitude]]** of enriched categories.
