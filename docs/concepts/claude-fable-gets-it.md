# Claude Fable & Karpathy's "Gets It"

## Insight
Claude Fable dropped June 9, 2026 and is, in the author's words, "basically Claude Mythos 5 with more cyber guardrails baked in." Mythos is the model Anthropic had been teasing for months — powerful enough that it wasn't generally available, only reachable to heavy cyber-and-infrastructure partners in project class swing. Fable is the public-facing release with the safeguards dialed up.

Karpathy's take (he had just joined Anthropic): "you can give Fable a lot more ambitious tasks than what you're used to and the model just *gets it*" — "gets it" in quotes, calling Fable "a major version bump deserving step change forward" and "a super exciting release." He flagged that the safeguards were configured "a little bit too trigger-happy for launch," which tracks with the author's experience of frequent "I can't do that" refusals. The benchmark is state-of-the-art; the *feel* is the bigger signal.

Cost reality: $10 per million input tokens and $50 per million output — 2x Opus. Available on subscription only June 9–22, then switches to usage credits. The author stress-tested it and burned through his entire $200/month max-plan 5-hour session limit in just over an hour. Two one-shot demos show what the model does when pointed at a real second brain: (1) a `/goal` prompt that produced a complete video about the author's journey, channel, and business in one pass; (2) another `/goal` that read every YouTube transcript in the vault and built a clickable interface mapping concepts (ideas, tools, techniques) to the videos they appeared in — finished in 21 minutes. Fable isn't cheap, isn't fast, and isn't unrestricted. But the "gets it" feel — multi-step reasoning actually landing on first pass — is the real signal.

## Context
From "I Turned Claude Into the Ultimate Second Brain" (Nate Herk, June 9, 2026); Fable release day, with the author using his own AIOS as the demo surface.
